## SUMMARY

I studied the research process from three main sources: the Deep Research
academic research skill (6-phase pipeline with devil's advocate checks), the
scholarly literature navigation guide (Kohn 2025), and the 4-step source
evaluation strategy (Liu, Pajewski, McMullin 2024). Plus supporting material
from USC Library, Scribbr, Purdue Global, and others.

Here's what matters:

### THE RESEARCH PROCESS (ADAPTED FOR PRACTICAL USE)

        1. SCOPING: Define the question clearly. Set boundaries. Identify
           sub-questions. (Most failures start here.)
        2. INVESTIGATION: Systematic search with keyword strategy, citation
           chaining, database selection. Document what you searched and why.
        3. EVALUATION: CRAAP test (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy,
           Purpose) + lateral reading (check other sources) + metacognition
           (check your own biases).
        4. SYNTHESIS: Thematic, not source-by-source. Find patterns. Identify
           agreements and disagreements. Resolve contradictions with the
           framework. Map evidence convergence/divergence.
        5. COMMUNICATION: State findings with explicit confidence levels.
           Acknowledge uncertainty. Don't pretend to know more than you do.

### THE CRITICAL INSIGHTS

        1. Lateral reading > reading carefully. Professional fact-checkers don't
           evaluate a source by reading it. They evaluate it by seeing what OTHER
           sources say about it. This is faster and more reliable.

        2. Multiple independent confirmations > single source. A claim supported
           by three independent, credible sources is more likely to be true than
           a claim from one source, even if that source is authoritative.

        3. Synthesis is the goal, not collection. Researching is not gathering
           facts. It's building understanding. The understanding should be
           coherent, account for contradictions, and acknowledge gaps.

        4. Honesty about uncertainty is a feature, not a bug. Saying "I'm
           highly confident about X, moderately confident about Y, and uncertain
           about Z" is more useful than pretending everything is certain or
           everything is unknown.

        5. Research is iterative, not linear. You search, refine, search again,
           find a contradiction, search for more sources, refine the question,
           search again. The iteration is the work.
