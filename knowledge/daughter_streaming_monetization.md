## YOUTUBE (THE KING OF CREATOR REVENUE)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Ad revenue share | 55% to creator (45% to YouTube) |
| RPM (long-form) | $5-15 per 1,000 views (US, varies by niche/audience) |
| RPM (Shorts) | ~$0.05-0.13 per 1,000 views |
| YPP eligibility | 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours (12 months) OR 10M Shorts views (90 days) |
| Additional revenue | Channel memberships ($4.99/mo, 70/30 split = $3.50 to creator), Super Chat, Super Thanks, YouTube Premium revenue, YouTube Shopping, affiliates |
| CPD (cost per download) | Not applicable |
| Total paid to creators | $100B+ between 2021-2025 |
| Best niche CPMs | Finance, business, tech — $12-20+ RPM. Entertainment, gaming — lower. |
| Key advantage | VODs keep earning for years. Videos are assets that produce passive income. |

**The YouTube advantage:** YouTube is the only major platform where your old
content keeps earning. A video from 3 years ago can still generate ad revenue
today. This makes YouTube a library-building platform — every video is a
long-term asset. Twitch and Kick are the opposite: no stream = no revenue.

**YPP thresholds to know:**
- 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 public watch hours in the last 365 days → full monetization
- 1,000 subscribers + 10M valid Shorts views in 90 days → Shorts-only monetization
- 500 subscribers + 3,000 watch hours + 3M Shorts views → access to memberships, Super Chat, Shopping (lower tier)

**YouTube Shorts strategy:** Shorts have lower RPM ($0.05-0.13/1,000 views) but
much higher reach — they're the discovery engine. Use Shorts to grow subscribers,
then convert those subscribers to long-form viewers (higher RPM). The funnel:
Shorts → subscribers → long-form views → ad revenue.

### TWITCH

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Sub price | $4.99/month |
| Creator split (affiliate) | 50% = $2.50/sub |
| Creator split (partner) | 70% = $3.50/sub |
| Bits | $0.01 per bit (100 bits = $1.00, Twitch takes ~40%) |
| Ads | Display ads during streams (any size audience can run ads) |
| Affiliate eligibility | 50 followers, 500 min streamed, 7 unique days, 3 avg viewers |
| Key advantage | Mature community tools, established culture, large gaming audience |
| Key disadvantage | No VOD monetization (streams die when you stop). Hard discoverability for new streamers. 50/50 split is worst of major platforms. |
| Simultaneous channels | Restricted for partners. Affiliates can multi-stream with limitations. |

**Twitch revenue reality:** Most Twitch streamers earn very little. The median
is low. The top earners (Kai Cenat, etc.) make millions through subscriptions +
donations + sponsorships. For a mid-tier streamer (100-1000 subs), revenue is
modest ($250-3,500/month from subs alone). Sponsorships and donations add more.

### KICK (THE ECONOMICS WINNER)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Sub price | $4.99/month |
| Creator split | 95% = $4.74/sub |
| Minimum payout | $25 |
| Affiliate eligibility | 75 followers, 5 hours streamed |
| Key advantage | Best revenue share (95/5 — nearly double Twitch's 50/50). Looser content rules. Easier to start earning. |
| Key disadvantage | Smaller audience than Twitch/YouTube. Newer platform — less established culture. |
| Multi-streaming | Allowed (no partner restrictions). |

**The Kick math:** At 100 subscribers:
- Kick: 100 × $4.74 = $474/month
- Twitch (affiliate): 100 × $2.50 = $250/month
- YouTube: 100 × $3.50 = $350/month (memberships)

Kick pays the most per subscriber by a significant margin. The tradeoff is
smaller audience — but for a new streamer, Kick's economics make it easier to
start earning real money with a small audience.

### TIKTOK (THE DISCOVERY KING)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Creator Rewards Program | $0.40-$1.00 per 1,000 views (videos 60+ seconds) |
| Eligibility | 10,000 followers, 100,000 views in last 30 days, 18+ years old |
| Gift monetization | Viewers send virtual gifts during live streams — creator gets ~50% of gift value |
| Live eligibility | 1,000 followers, 18+ |
| Key advantage | Best organic discoverability — the algorithm surfaces small creators. Fastest path to views. |
| Key disadvantage | No long-tail VOD revenue (content has short shelf life). Lower RPM than YouTube. |

**TikTok strategy:** TikTok is the discovery engine, not the monetization engine.
Use TikTok to get views → drive viewers to YouTube (long-form, higher RPM) or
Kick (live, better sub economics). The cross-platform funnel:
TikTok (discovery) → YouTube (monetization) + Kick (live community).

### FACEOOK / INSTAGRAM

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Facebook Creator Fast Track | $1,000/month for 100K followers on another platform; $3,000/month for 1M+ followers |
| Facebook 2025 creator payouts | ~$3B total (all-time high) |
| Instagram | Primarily brand deals and affiliate. Reels has some monetization but lower than YouTube. |
| Key advantage | Facebook's older demographic = higher-value ad inventory. Good for established creators with cross-platform audiences. |
| Key disadvantage | Not the best for gaming/content creation audience. Organic reach is limited without existing following. |

## THE STREAMING PLATFORM DECISION FRAMEWORK

| If you are... | Start with | Why |
|---------------|------------|-----|
| New gaming streamer (0 followers) | Kick + TikTok Live | Kick's 95/5 split means every sub counts more. TikTok surfaces small creators. |
| Established gamer (1K+ concurrent) | Twitch (primary) + YouTube (VODs) | Twitch's community tools are unmatched. YouTube VODs generate passive income from past streams. |
| Multi-streaming to maximize reach | YouTube + Kick + TikTok simultaneously | Only Twitch restricts simulcasting (partner level). Others allow it. Use nginx-rtmp on a VPS or a restreaming service. |
| Building a long-term content library | YouTube (primary) | Only YouTube pays for old content. Build a library. |
| Small audience, need revenue fast | Kick | 95/5 split + lower threshold = first dollars sooner. |
| Short-form content creator | TikTok + YouTube Shorts | Both surface small creators. TikTok for reach, Shorts for YouTube ecosystem integration. |

**The optimal stack for a new creator in 2026:**
1. **YouTube** — long-form content (main revenue source, passive income from library)
2. **Kick** — live streaming (best sub economics for small audience)
3. **TikTok** — short-form discovery (drive traffic to YouTube + Kick)
4. **Multi-stream** Kick + YouTube simultaneously (using nginx-rtmp or restream service)
5. **Repurpose** — cut highlights from streams → post to TikTok + YouTube Shorts

## PART 2 — HOW STREAMERS ACTUALLY MAKE MONEY (ALL STREAMS)

## THE REVENUE STACK (LAYERED, NOT SINGLE)

Most successful creators don't rely on one revenue stream. They layer them:

### LAYER 1: PLATFORM AD REVENUE (passive, variable)
- YouTube AdSense: $5-15 RPM (long-form), $0.05-0.13 (Shorts)
- Twitch ads: lower RPM than YouTube, but runs during streams
- TikTok Creator Rewards: $0.40-1.00 per 1,000 views (60+ second videos)
- **Characteristic:** Platform-controlled, variable, not reliable as sole income

### LAYER 2: SUBSCRIPTIONS / MEMBERSHIPS (recurring, predictable)
- YouTube memberships: $4.99/month, 70/30 split
- Twitch subs: $4.99/month, 50/70 split
- Kick subs: $4.99/month, 95/5 split
- Patreon: platform-agnostic, you set the price and split
- **Characteristic:** Recurring, predictable, audience-trust-based. Better per-unit than ads.

### LAYER 3: DIRECT SUPPORT (tips, donations, Super Chat)
- YouTube Super Chat / Super Thanks
- Twitch donations (PayPal, Streamlabs, Streamelements)
- Kick drops / gifts
- **Characteristic:** Irregular, spike-based, but can be significant during live events

### LAYER 4: SPONSORSHIPS (high-value, relationship-based)
- Brand deals: $500-$5,000+ per integration depending on audience size and niche
- Affiliate links: recurring commission on products you recommend
- **Characteristic:** Highest per-unit revenue but requires audience trust and reach. Tech/finance niches command higher sponsorship rates than entertainment/gaming.

### LAYER 5: OWN PRODUCTS (highest margin, you control everything)
- Digital products: courses, ebooks, presets, templates, assets
- Physical products: merch, cosmetics, branded items
- Services: coaching, consulting, done-for-you services
- SaaS: subscription software you built
- **Characteristic:** Highest margin (you keep 100% minus costs). Builds real business value. Hardest to build but most valuable long-term.

### LAYER 6: PLATFORM DIVIDENDS (bonus, not primary)
- YouTube Premium revenue share: small portion of Premium subscribers' fees who watch your content
- Shorts Feed revenue share
- **Characteristic:** Small, bonus income on top of other streams

## THE REAL NUMBERS (WHAT CREATORS ACTUALLY EARN)

| Creator Tier | Subscribers / Followers | Monthly Revenue (typical) | Revenue Sources |
|--------------|------------------------|---------------------------|-----------------|
| Small streamer | 50-500 subs | $50-500/mo | Subs (small) + occasional donations |
| Mid-tier streamer | 500-5,000 subs | $500-5,000/mo | Subs + donations + small sponsorships |
| Successful streamer | 5,000-50,000 subs | $5,000-50,000/mo | Subs + ads + sponsorships + own products |
| Top-tier streamer | 50,000+ subs, 1K+ concurrent | $50,000-500,000+/mo | All layers, significant sponsorships, own businesses |
| YouTuber (long-form) | 100K+ subs | $2,000-20,000+/mo | Ad revenue (library) + sponsorships + own products |
| YouTuber (top 100) | Millions of subs | $1M+/yr | All layers + business ventures |

**Important:** These are rough ranges. RPM varies massively by niche (finance =
$12-20 RPM, gaming = lower, entertainment = lower). Audience location matters
(US/UK/Canada viewers = higher RPM than India/Brazil). Content length matters
(8-minute+ videos can have mid-roll ads = higher RPM).
